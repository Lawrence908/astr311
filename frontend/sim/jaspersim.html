Integration

Yoshida 4th-order symplectic integrator. Symplectic integrators preserve the Hamiltonian structure of the equations of motion, meaning energy and angular momentum remain bounded over long timescales — unlike standard Runge-Kutta which accumulates drift. Three force evaluations per timestep give 4th-order accuracy.

Force Law

Plummer-softened Newtonian gravity plus the Einstein–Infeld–Hoffmann (EIH) 1st Post-Newtonian correction. The softening parameter ε = 0.001 AU prevents singularities at close approach without affecting dynamics at orbital separations. The 1PN term reproduces Mercury's 43.00″/century perihelion precession — a direct test of General Relativity.

Collisions

Perfectly inelastic mergers triggered when two bodies' visual radii overlap. Mass, momentum, and volume are all conserved: new radius = ∛(r₁³ + r₂³), new velocity = (m₁v₁ + m₂v₂) / (m₁ + m₂). Mergers are applied after the full Yoshida step to preserve the symplectic integrator structure.

Spacetime Mesh

The curvature mesh is a Flamm paraboloid — the standard embedding diagram of the Schwarzschild metric. Each body warps the grid by z ∝ −2√(r_s · r) where r_s = 2GM/c² is the Schwarzschild radius. The mesh is purely visual; it does not feed back into the physics. Warp scale adjusts visual depth, mesh extent adjusts the diameter of the grid plane.
